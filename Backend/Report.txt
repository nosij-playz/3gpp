    Query: What is the role of the AMF in 5G?
    Status: success
    Elapsed: 10.57s
    Answer:
        The Access and Mobility Management function (AMF) plays a crucial role in 5G networks. Its main functionalities include:
        
        - Termination of RAN CP interface (N2)
        - Termination of NAS (N1), NAS ciphering and integrity protection
        - Registration management
        - Connection management
        
        The AMF is responsible for managing the mobility of UEs, including registration, authentication, and connection management. It also provides the 5G-AN node with the weight factors for each TNL association of the AMF and can request the 5G-AN node to add or remove TNL associations to the AMF.
        
        The AMF selection functionality is supported by the 5G-AN (e.g. RAN, N3IWF) and is used to select an AMF instance for a given UE. The AMF selection functionality in the 5G-AN may consider the following factors for selecting the AMF Set:
        
        - AMF Region ID and AMF Set ID derived from GUAMI
        - Requested NSSAI
        - Local operator policies
        - 5G CIoT features indicated in RRC signalling by the UE
        - IAB-indication
        - NB-IoT RAT Type
        - Category M Indication
        - NR RedCap Indication
        
        The AMF also supports the indication to the 5G-AN node of the set of TNL associations used for UE-associated signalling and the set of TNL associations used for non-UE associated signalling.
        
        **AMF Functionalities:**
        
        | Functionality | Description |
        | --- | --- |
        | Termination of RAN CP interface (N2) | Termination of RAN CP interface |
        | Termination of NAS (N1), NAS ciphering and integrity protection | Termination of NAS, ciphering, and integrity protection |
        | Registration management | Management of UE registration |
        | Connection management | Management of UE connections |
        
        **AMF Selection Factors:**
        
        | Factor | Description |
        | --- | --- |
        | AMF Region ID and AMF Set ID derived from GUAMI | Derived from Global User Ambient Identity (GUAMI) |
        | Requested NSSAI | Network Slice Selection Assistance Information (NSSAI) requested by the UE |
        | Local operator policies | Policies defined by the local operator |
        | 5G CIoT features indicated in RRC signalling by the UE | 5G CIoT features indicated in Radio Resource Control (RRC) signalling |

    Query: What are the main functions of the gNB?
    Status: success
    Elapsed: 13.84s
    Answer:
        The main functions of the gNB are:
        
        - Functions for Radio Resource Management: 
          - Radio Bearer Control
          - Radio Admission Control
          - Connection Mobility Control
          - Dynamic allocation of resources to UEs in uplink, downlink and sidelink (scheduling)
        
        - IP and Ethernet header compression, uplink data decompression, encryption and integrity protection of data
        
        Additionally, the gNB:
        
        - Provides the intended service area information to the UEs in the relevant cell(s) as defined in TS 38.331 [12]
        - Implements the NAS Node Selection Function specified in TS 38.410 [16]
        - Allocates and co-ordinates the usage of A-IoT radio resources
        - Confirms the request from the A-IoT CN node by replying with the Inventory Response message
        - Is connected via the NG interface to the 5GC, more specifically to the AMF (Access and Mobility Management Function) by means of the NG-C interface and to the UPF (User Plane Function) by means of the NG-U interface (see TS 23.501 [3])
        
        The gNB may also be subdivided into non-NTN infrastructure gNB functions and the NTN Service Link provisioning system, which may consist of one or more NTN payloads and NTN Gateways.

    Query: What is the N2 interface?
    Status: success
    Elapsed: 19.44s
    Answer:
        The N2 interface is a control plane interface between the 5G-AN (5G Access Network) and the 5G Core. It maps to the NG-C interface as defined in TS 38.413 [34]. The N2 interface is used for procedures related to N2 Interface Management that are not related to an individual UE, such as configuration or reset of the N2 interface.
        
        The N2 interface is supported by the following protocol stacks:
        
        - NGAP (Next Generation Access and Mobility Protocol)
        - SCTP (Stream Control Transmission Protocol)
        - IP (Internet Protocol)
        
        The N2 interface can be terminated at MWAB-gNB and UE-AMF, and it is optionally used as a concentrator for the N2 interface by the NR Femto GW. The NR Femto GW appears to the AMF as a gNB and to the NR Femto node(s) as an AMF.
        
        The N2 interface is used for the following procedures:
        
        - Configuration of the N2 interface
        - Reset of the N2 interface
        - Other N2 Interface Management procedures not related to an individual UE
        
        The N2 interface is secured by security mechanisms defined in TS 38.300 [27] and TS 38.413 [34].

    Query: Describe the 5G registration procedure.
    Status: success
    Elapsed: 31.23s
    Answer:
        The 5G registration procedure involves the following steps:
        
        1. **N5GC Device Connection**: The N5GC device connects to the W-AGF via a RG which is configured as a layer 2 bridge. The W-AGF is associated with a 5GC and acts on behalf of the N5GC device during its registration process.
        
        2. **Registration Procedure**: The registration procedure for the 5G-RG connecting to 5GC via NG-RAN is specified in TS 23.316 [79] clause 4.11. The registration procedure for the 5G-RG connecting to 5GC via W-5GAN is specified in TS 23.316 [79] clause 7.2.1.
        
        3. **Authentication**: The 5G-RG shall support both 5G-AKA and EAP-AKA’ and it shall be authenticated by the 3GPP home network.
        
        4. **Temporary Identifier Allocation**: A temporary identifier (5G-GUTI) is allocated to support user confidentiality protection.
        
        5. **Permanent Identifier Allocation**: A globally unique 5G Subscription Permanent Identifier (SUPI) is allocated to each subscriber in the 5G System and provisioned in the UDM/UDR.
        
        **Registration Procedure Details**:
        
        | Registration Method | Clause Reference |
        | --- | --- |
        | NG-RAN | TS 23.316 [79] clause 4.11 |
        | W-5GAN | TS 23.316 [79] clause 7.2.1 |
        
        **Authentication Methods**:
        
        | Authentication Method | Supported |
        | --- | --- |
        | 5G-AKA | Yes |
        | EAP-AKA’ | Yes |
        
        **Identifier Allocation**:
        
        | Identifier Type | Allocation Method |
        | --- | --- |
        | 5G-GUTI | Temporary allocation for user confidentiality protection |
        | SUPI | Globally unique identifier allocated to each subscriber in the 5G System and provisioned in the UDM/UDR |

        - Termination of RAN CP interface (N2);
        - Access and Mobility Management Function (AMF);

        - Unstructured Data Storage Function (UDSF);
        - Network Exposure Function (NEF);
        - Network Repository Function (NRF);
        - Network Slice Admission Control Function (NSACF);
        - Network Slice-specific and SNPN Authentication and Authorization Function (NSSAAF);
        - Network Slice Selection Function (NSSF);
        - Policy Control Function (PCF).
        
        The AMF uses the N14 interface for AMF re-allocation and AMF to AMF information transfer, which may be either intra-PLMN or inter-PLMN.

        **5G Security Overview**
        
        The 5G security context is established locally at the UE and a serving network domain, represented by the "5G security context data" stored at the UE and a serving network. This context consists of two main components:
        
        * 5G NAS security context
        * 5G AS security context for 3GPP access and/or 5G AS security context for non-3GPP access
        
        **5G AS Secondary Cell Security Context**
        
        The 5G AS Secondary Cell security context includes:
        
        * Cryptographic keys at AS level for secondary cell with their identifiers
        * Identifier of the selected AS level cryptographic algorithms for secondary cell
        * UP Security Policy at the network side
        * Counters used for replay protection
        
        **5G Home Environment Authentication Vector**
        
        The 5G Home Environment Authentication Vector consists of:
        
        * RAND (Random Number)
        * AUTN (Authentication Token)
        * XRES* (Expected Response)
        * KAUSF (Key Agreement User-Specific Key)
        
        **Security Entities in the 5G Core Network**
        
        The 5G System architecture introduces the following security entities in the 5G Core network:
        
        | Security Entity | Description |
        | --- | --- |
        | SEPP (Security Edge Protection Proxy) | Entity sitting at the perimeter of the PLMN |
        | AUSF (Authentication Server Function) | AUthentication Server Function |
        
        **5G Security Architecture**
        
        The 5G security architecture includes the following components:
        
        * UE (User Equipment)
        * Serving Network
        * 5G Core Network
        * Security Edge Protection Proxy (SEPP)
        * Authentication Server Function (AUSF)
        
        **5G Security Procedures**
        
        Existing security procedures defined in this document are reused for 5G-RG (Radio Access Network) connections via wireline access network (W-5GAN) or via Fixed Wireless Access (FWA).

[18] ID: s1 | Category: Stress
    Query: What is 5G?
    Status: success
    Elapsed: 40.75s
    Answer:
        The 5G System provides data connectivity and services. This specification covers both roaming and non-roaming scenarios in all aspects, including interworking between 5GS and EPS, mobility within 5GS, QoS, policy control and charging, authentication and in general 5G System wide features e.g. SMS, Location Services, Emergency Services.
        
        The 5G architecture is defined as service-based and the interaction between network functions is represented in two ways.
        
        - A service-based representation, where network functions (e.g. AMF) within the Control Plane enables other authorized network functions to access their services. This representation also includes point-to-point reference points where necessary.
        
        A 5G-RG is a RG capable of connecting to 5GC playing the role of a UE with regard to the 5G core. It supports secure element and exchanges N1 signalling with 5GC. The 5G-RG can be either a 5G-BRG or 5G-CRG.
        
        Access Traffic Steering is the procedure that selects an access network for a new service data flow and transfers the traffic of this service data flow over the selected access network.

    Query: How does beamforming work in 5G?
    Status: success
    Elapsed: 49.13s
    Answer:
        Based on the provided context, I will provide information about beamforming in 5G.
        
        Beamforming is a technique used in 5G to improve the quality of the wireless connection between the user equipment (UE) and the base station (gNB). The UE is configured to consider a subset of the detected beams, and filtering takes place at two different levels: at the physical layer to derive beam quality and then at RRC level to derive cell quality from multiple beams.
        
        Here is a high-level measurement model describing how beamforming works in 5G:
        
        - **Measurements (beam specific samples)**: internal to the physical layer (A).
        - **Layer 1 filtering**: internal layer 1 filtering of the inputs measured at point A (implementation dependent).
        - **Measurements (i.e. beam specific measurements)**: reported by layer 1 to layer 3 after layer 1 filtering (A1).
        - **Beam Consolidation/Selection**: beam specific measurements are consolidated to derive cell quality (B).
        - **Layer 3 filtering for cell quality**: filtering performed on the measurements provided at point B (C).
        - **Evaluation of reporting criteria**: checks whether actual measurement reporting is necessary (D).
        
        The UE is configured to consider a subset of the detected beams, and measurement reports may contain the measurement results of the X best beams if the UE is configured to do so by the gNB.
        
        **Key terms related to beamforming in 5G:**
        
        - **Beam quality**: derived at the physical layer.
        - **Cell quality**: derived from multiple beams at the RRC level.
        - **Beam Consolidation/Selection**: standardised and configured by RRC signalling.
        - **Layer 3 filters**: standardised and configured by RRC signalling.
